# 2026-10-30 — compensating events for cash

## What changed

1. Two new events: `DepositReversed` and `WithdrawalReversed`, each with `correctsSeq` and the amount copied from the original.
2. `Stored<E> = E & { seq }`: what comes out of the store now carries its position in the stream. Both plugs return it; `append` is unchanged.
3. `Account` gained `reversedSeqs`. The fold nets a reversal against its original and remembers the position.
4. Two verbs, `reverseDeposit` and `reverseWithdrawal`, taking the original event as the business input.
5. Two commands, `reverseDeposit(deps, seq)` and `reverseWithdrawal(deps, seq)`.
6. Two routes, `POST /api/deposits/:seq/reverse` and `POST /api/withdrawals/:seq/reverse`.
7. UI: a Reverse button on every deposit and withdrawal not yet reversed; reversed rows struck through with "reversed by #N"; reversal rows read "reverses #N · amount"; the `#` column is the real `seq`.
8. Tests: 53, up from 43.

Trades are deliberately left out. Reversing a buy must answer what happens to the average cost and to any sale after it. That is its own discussion.

## Why it is shaped this way

### A wrong event stays; a new event says it did not count

This is the lead's stance made concrete. Nothing is edited or deleted. A reversal is a past-tense fact in the same stream as the thing it corrects, with a pointer back. A reader of the stream sees both, in order, without a lookup, because the amount is copied onto the reversal.

### The pointer is the position, for now

A reversal must point at the event it reverses. Events carry the account id, not their own, so the only identity an event has is its place in the stream: `seq`, which the store assigns. The store now hands it back:

```ts
export type Stored<E> = E & { seq: number };
```

This does not reopen the id debate. `seq` is the store's row number, not a minted identity; no verb ever sets it. It is the generic the lead mentioned ("real TS"), and it is honest: the domain works on stored events, with their position, which is "all logic on stored events" taken literally.

**To check with the lead:** whether events should get their own ids back. If so, the change is contained: `correctsSeq` becomes `correctsId`, the verbs take the original by id, and `Stored<E>` either goes or keeps `seq` as extra information. Rules, fold shape and tests do not change.

### The original event is the business input

`reverseDeposit(account, original: Stored<DepositCollectedEvent>, now)`. Same shape as every other verb: aggregate, input, time. The input is the event being reversed, the way an order is the input for a buy. The parameter type makes "reverse a deposit that is really a withdrawal" impossible to write; the compiler refuses it before any rule runs.

### One rule: a reversal happens once

`Account.reversedSeqs` is the state that rule needs, so it joined the aggregate, the same way positions joined when "cannot sell what you do not hold" appeared. A second reversal of the same event is refused with the position in the message.

### A reversed deposit may leave the balance negative

If the money was already spent, reversing the deposit drives cash below zero. The verb allows it. The fact is the fact, and the lead's own line was that the invariant is having a balance, not a positive one. There is a test asserting this, so the decision is visible.

### Finding the original is a lookup, not a rule

The command reads the stream, finds the event at `seq`, and fails if there is none or it is the wrong kind: "no deposit at position 7". That is address resolution, so it may live in the app layer. Two commands rather than one `reverse` with an `if` on the type, for the usual reason.

## Verified live

Deposit $123.45 became event #11. Reversing it appended #12, `DepositReversed` correcting 11, and the balance went back by $123.45 with `reversedSeqs: [11]`. Reversing #11 again: "deposit #11 was already reversed". Reversing #2, a buy, as a deposit: "no deposit at position 2". A non-numeric seq: refused at the edge.

## Tests (53, no mocks)

- Domain: a reversal points at the original and copies its amount; a deposit can be reversed once; the fold nets an event against its reversal and remembers the position; reversing after the money was spent is allowed.
- Commands: reversal nets the balance and the original stays in the stream; reversing twice fails and appends nothing; a position that is not a deposit, or does not exist, cannot be reversed as one; reversing a withdrawal returns the cash.
- Contract: `seq` counts from 1 per stream, in both stores.

## Open for the next review

1. Event ids: keep `seq` as the pointer, or give events their own identity back?
2. Should a reversal of a deposit that drives the balance negative be allowed, as built, or refused with a message?
3. Reversing trades: what is the rule for average cost and for sales that happened after the reversed buy?
4. `reversedSeqs` on the aggregate versus checking the stream in the command. The aggregate was chosen because "once" is a rule.
5. The UI confirms before reversing. Enough, or should a reversal require a reason that goes on the event?
