# 2026-10-28 — prices from Alpaca: the socket, two plugs, market orders

## What changed

1. A `PriceSource` interface in the domain.
2. Two implementations in a new `src/marketdata/` folder: `AlpacaPriceSource` and `FixedPriceSource`.
3. `Deps` gained `prices`; `main` picks the plug from the environment.
4. `buy` and `sell` fetch the price instead of trusting a typed one. They take a `MarketOrder` and became `async`.
5. `GET /api/prices/:symbol`; the order body lost `priceCents`; `reply` awaits.
6. `InMemoryEventStore`, a second store implementation, so commands can be tested.
7. Tests: 41, up from 22. Command tests, a store contract suite, price-source tests.
8. `.env` created (ignored by git) with `APCA_API_KEY_ID` and `APCA_API_SECRET_KEY`; `npm run dev` loads it.

## Why it is shaped this way

### A price is an input, not a fact of the account

A price is something the market says, not something the account did. So it is
not an `AccountEvent`, it has no invariant, and it is not an aggregate. It
enters the system through a socket and is handed to a verb as part of the
order. The fact that *is* recorded is still `StockBought` / `StockSold` with
its `priceCents`: the price a trade happened at belongs on the trade.

### The three layers

| Layer | What it holds | Files |
|---|---|---|
| domain | the socket, a pure type | `domain/priceSource.ts` |
| app | fetches the price, hands it to the verb | `commands/account.ts` (`priceOrder`, `buy`, `sell`) |
| infrastructure | the plugs that know a vendor or a table | `marketdata/alpacaPriceSource.ts`, `marketdata/fixedPriceSource.ts` |

The domain did not change at all. `buyStock(account, order, now)` still takes
a `StockOrder` with its price. That was the test of the design: adding a
vendor touched nothing that holds a business rule.

### Network rules in the Alpaca plug

Every call has a five-second timeout. Every failure is a `Result`, never a
throw: unreachable provider, rejected keys, unexpected status, no recent
trade. The vendor's JSON is checked with zod, because it comes from outside
the world. The provider's message passes through to the user unchanged
(decided: honest beats soft).

### The second plug is not a mock

`FixedPriceSource` answers from a table. It is a real implementation of the
interface that happens to be simple. The lead's objection to mocking is to
asserting on *how* something was called; a second plug tests *what* comes
out. Command tests use it together with `InMemoryEventStore`, which is the
same idea for the store.

### Fetch only

A typed price was removed from the order body. If it is sent anyway, zod
strips it. A journal entry "after the fact" would be an optional field later;
not building it until asked.

## Why the names

- **`PriceSource`, not `QuoteSource`.** The method is `latestPriceCents` and every event field is `priceCents`; one word throughout. "Quote" in a broker's mouth means a bid and an ask, which we do not model.
- **`MarketOrder`** `{ symbol, quantity }`: the business word for "this many at whatever the market says". No price named, filled at the current price.
- **`StockOrder`** `{ symbol, quantity, priceCents }` stays in the domain: the order with its price attached, what the verb decides on. Both are value objects.
- **No `side` field on `MarketOrder`.** The order describes *what*; the verb (`buy` or `sell`) says *which action*. A `side` field would mean one `trade` command with an `if` on it, which is business logic in the app layer. The HTTP body does carry `side`, but only so one route can serve two buttons: translating `side` into a choice of command is the edge's job.
- **`priceOrder`**: attach the price to an order. The one helper both commands share.

## Why there is no `prices` event stream

Proposed: a second stream of `PriceFetched` events. Declined for now, with the reasoning:

- The question "what price did we trade at" is already answered by the trade events.
- The question "what is the price now" is one call, and for the report it is a cache table on the read side with a `fetched_at`.
- A stream is where an aggregate's facts live, and an aggregate is the representation of an invariant. A price has no invariant, no verb, nothing to protect. It would be a log wearing a stream's name.
- `PriceFetched` is infrastructure describing itself, not a sentence a trader says.
- The store is typed to `AccountEvent`; a second event type means `EventStore<E>`, a cost paid for a question nobody has asked.
- Polled prices grow the log with events nobody acted on, and bad quotes would stay forever.

What would change the answer: a backward-looking valuation report ("what was the portfolio worth last Tuesday") or deterministic replay without the network. Then price history is a real need and a stream is the right tool.

Rule to apply to any proposed stream: name the verb that produces its events and the rule it protects. If neither exists, it is a table.

## Tests (41, no mocks)

- `tests/commands.test.ts`: deposit and withdraw round-trip; buy records the fetched price and spends that much; sell at a moved price returns cash; unknown symbol fails with the provider's message and appends nothing; the fetched price can make an order unaffordable; selling what is not held fails.
- `tests/eventStore.contract.test.ts`: five assertions run against the SQL store on `:memory:` and the in-memory store.
- `tests/priceSource.test.ts`: the fixed plug answers known and unknown symbols.
- The Alpaca plug has no unit test: infrastructure is proven by running it. It was: AAPL at $347.10, MSFT at $531.58 recorded on real events.

## Verified live

Deposit, buy 2 AAPL at market, sell 1, buy an unknown symbol (provider 404
passed through), buy 1000 NVDA (insufficient cash at the fetched price). The
page shows the current price under the order form before you trade and reads
the fill price back from the newest event after.

## Open for the next review

1. An unknown symbol now fails with the provider's 404, because the price is fetched before the verb runs. Checking `isKnownStock` first in the command would save a network call but move a domain rule into the app layer. Left as is.
2. The price on the event is fetched a few milliseconds before the fold. Same timing question as two concurrent withdrawals; same answer, an expected `seq` on append.
3. `PriceFetched` stream: history of prices as facts, or is the price on each trade enough?
4. A journal entry with a typed price, after the fact: wanted or not?
